30 MHz Class-E Inverter

MIT Power Electronics Research Group · Research

I designed and simulated a Class-E inverter targeting 500 W, laid out its PCB in Altium with guidance from my research supervisor, and evaluated its performance in the lab.

Measured and simulated results

EvidenceResult
Design target500 W at 30 MHz
Simulation85% efficiency at 500 W output
Lab measurement84% efficiency at 350 W output

The simulation and measurement values are at different output powers; they are not a same-operating-point correlation. I also evaluated the output-power range achievable with a modulation strategy.

Lab measurements

I measured output power with a V–I probe and used high-frequency probes and SMA connections to observe gate-drive and drain waveforms. The evaluation included soft-switching behavior, efficiency, and device temperature.

This work went beyond measuring one output value: I used waveform behavior to guide tuning as the operating voltage changed.

Frequency and resonant-tank tuning

A key challenge was the voltage dependence of the GaN device’s output capacitance. A tuning point established at low voltage did not automatically carry over as voltage increased. I adjusted switching frequency in voltage steps while checking the switching waveforms.

I also used impedance measurements to tune the input resonant tank and account for high-frequency parasitics. Because the impedance instrument operated at low voltage, I iterated between those measurements and powered circuit tests.
